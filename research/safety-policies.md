# Member safety: research notes

## Method
Searches and fetches conducted 2026-10-05 across dating apps (Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, OkCupid), Meetup.com, and Australian official sources.

## Themes

The following safety advice themes appear consistently across dating apps (Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, OkCupid, Match Group), Meetup.com, and Australian authorities:

- **Meet in public first:** All sources emphasize choosing busy, populated locations for first meetings. Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, OkCupid all recommend public venues like cafés or parks. eSafety warns against home or isolated spots. Victoria Police note the importance of "lots of people around."

- **Tell a trusted friend:** Tinder's "Share My Date" feature, OkCupid, Hinge, and eSafety all recommend informing a friend or family member of your plans, location, and time. Victoria Police suggest sharing location with friends.

- **Own transport / plan arrival:** OkCupid and Bumble stress arranging your own transport to/from the meeting. Victoria Police recommends having a friend drop you off/pick you up or arranging check-in calls.

- **Video call before meeting:** Tinder, Bumble, and Hinge all recommend video calls to confirm identity before meeting in person. OkCupid and Hinge note that refusal to video call is a red flag.

- **Don't share personal information early:** All apps warn against sharing home address, workplace, phone number, financial details, or children's information before establishing trust in person. Bumble suggests keeping profile details vague.

- **Trust your instincts / set boundaries:** Tinder, OkCupid, and eSafety all emphasize trusting gut feelings and leaving if something feels wrong. Tinder and OkCupid stress the right to end a date early.

- **Report and block:** Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, OkCupid all provide block/report tools for suspicious or harassing users. Meetup.com requires respecting blocks and bans.

- **Watch for scams and red flags:** All apps warn about romance scams, requests for money, stories too good to be true, and pressure to move conversations off-platform. Specific red flags: unwillingness to meet or video call, only one or two photos, pushing for serious relationship too fast, vague answers.

- **Avoid alcohol impairment:** OkCupid explicitly warns against anything impairing judgment. RMIT's makerspace safety notes not using equipment if under the influence.

- **Meet during daylight:** Tinder recommends daytime for added safety. Victoria Police note "during daylight hours."

- **Verify who they are:** Bumble, Hinge, and OkCupid recommend photo verification badges, social media checks, or reverse image searches. Hinge notes verification has limits.

## Age policies

**Minimum age requirement:** All dating apps (Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, OkCupid) and Meetup.com require users to be 18 years old. Match Group states it "asks for your birthdate to make sure you're at least 18 years old."

**Age verification methods:**
- **Tinder:** Uses face-based age estimation (required). In some regions (e.g., UK), ID verification is mandatory. Accepted IDs: driver's license or passport. ID details kept 30–90 days after verification. Optional "ID + Photo" badge available in US (includes registered sex offender check). Face estimation alone is the common path; ID is triggered by region or account flags.
- **Bumble:** Photo verification is mandatory in the USA. All active accounts linked to a phone number. In some regions (e.g., South Korea), age verification via government ID is required, processed by third-party Veriff with opt-in biometric processing.
- **Hinge:** Searches mention "Selfie Verified" badge but do not confirm mandatory ID checks. Reliance on self-reported birthdate and photo verification.
- **OkCupid:** Self-reported birthdate in account setup. Photo verification mentioned as optional feature with blue checkmark badge.
- **Match Group (general):** Asks for birthdate; states it checks for underage accounts proactively.

**Note on verification scope:** Most apps do not require government ID for all users; it is typically mandatory only in specific regions (e.g., UK for Tinder) or when triggered by account review. Self-declaration combined with photo/face verification is the standard path.

## Minors with a parent or guardian

**Meetup.com policy (the only platform with explicit guidance):**
- Meetup members and organizers must be 18+. No exceptions: minors cannot have their own accounts even with parental consent.
- Children can attend events: Children are welcome at family-oriented Meetup events if accompanied and supervised by an adult guardian the entire time.
- Groups must be 18+: Groups are banned if "targeted to individuals under 18." All groups are marketed to adults only.
- How it works in practice: A parent/guardian (18+) creates an account and RSVPs. Their children attend the event with them, supervised throughout.
- Example enforcement: "Parents With Young Children" groups note "not a drop-off event" and "Parents with Teens Social Group" requires all under-18 attendees to have a guardian present.

**Dating apps:** No guidance found. Dating apps (Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, OkCupid) are adults-only platforms with 18+ membership. The context of supervised attendance does not arise.

**eSafety Commissioner:** No specific guidance on minors attending adult online-to-offline meetups with a parent. The Commissioner covers child safety and adult cyber abuse but does not address this scenario.

## Australian resources

**eSafety Commissioner (esafety.gov.au):**
- **Role:** Independent regulator for online safety in Australia. Originally focused on child safety (2015); expanded to all Australians (2017). Current Commissioner: Julie Inman Grant.
- **Adult Cyber Abuse Scheme (commenced 23 January 2022):** Provides recourse for Australians 18+ targeted by serious online abuse (menacing, harassing, or offensive; intended to cause serious harm). eSafety can order removal of material and impose civil penalties (fines) if service providers do not comply.
- **Online dating safety advice** (esafety.gov.au/key-topics/staying-safe/online-dating):
  - Trust your gut; look for warning signs of abuse.
  - Meet in public first (café, pub, service station where you can walk away safely).
  - Avoid meeting at home (difficult to ask someone to leave) or isolated spots (no one nearby if trouble occurs).
  - Warn of last-minute venue changes.
  - Tell a friend/family member where you're going and when. Arrange for a friend to drop off/pick up, or a check-in call at an agreed time.
  - Check privacy settings on public accounts.
  - Report serious abuse to eSafety and seek support (image-based abuse, sextortion).
- **Notes:** eSafety enforces an industry code of practice for dating but has no legislative powers over it. eSafety does not regulate dating apps' own safety features (e.g., verification, reporting tools).

**Victoria Police (police.vic.gov.au):**
- **Safety plan for meeting strangers online:**
  - Meet somewhere busy with lots of people around.
  - Meet during daylight hours.
  - Share your location with friends.
  - Know the app's community guidelines and terms of service before signing up.
  - Check how the platform will support you if you encounter an issue.
- **Context:** Victoria Police note that reports of sexual assault after meeting someone in person who was first met online have increased. The advice applies to any online meeting context (dating, classified ads, etc.).

**Other mentions:** No other Australian government or health department resources on online safety for adults meeting online were found in searches.

## Hands-on / workshop safety

Australian makerspaces and university workshops consistently require safety induction before tool use:

**UNSW Making Network (Sydney & Canberra):**
- Mandatory Workshop Safety Induction (WS badge); quiz must achieve 100% to pass.
- Requirements: Closed shoes, no loose clothing/jewelry, safe conduct at all times.
- Use equipment only if authorized and trained; follow Safe Work Procedures (SWPs) at each machine.
- Non-compliance may result in suspension of access.

**RMIT Makerspace (Melbourne):**
- Compulsory safety induction and training for all users.
- Duty of care: You are responsible for your own and others' health and safety while in the workshop.
- Rules: No eating/drinking while working. Don't use equipment if tired, distracted, under the influence of alcohol or drugs. Don't distract other makers.

**University of Wollongong Engineering Workshop:**
- General workshop safety induction before any use.
- Machine-specific induction before using any equipment.

**City of Canada Bay Open Maker (NSW public library):**
- Four key principles: Check environment for hazards, check equipment, check yourself, ask staff if unsure.
- Requirements: Enclosed shoes mandatory. Tie back loose hair, clothing, jewelry. Report damaged equipment.
- Specific warnings (e.g., 3D printers: bed >40°C, nozzle >200°C).

**Gosford Regional Library MakerSpace (NSW public library):**
- Mandatory induction for all participants.
- Eligibility: Age 16+, library membership, photo ID required, enclosed shoes.
- Covers rules and emergency procedures (no step-by-step machine training).
- Induction must be redone annually.

**Common patterns across all Australian makerspaces:**
- Two-tier induction: general space safety, then machine-specific training.
- Personal protective equipment: closed shoes mandatory; tie back loose items.
- Equipment use: only after authorized training; follow posted SWPs.
- Workspace responsibility: keep clean, report hazards, don't use damaged equipment.
- Emergency awareness: know procedures and emergency contacts (e.g., call 000).
- Refresher requirements: some spaces require annual reinduction.

## Gaps

- **eSafety Commissioner's exact advice page:** The main eSafety online dating page (esafety.gov.au/key-topics/staying-safe/online-dating) was not fully fetched due to timeout; information comes from search snippets and QNews reporting of eSafety's advice. Direct access would confirm exact wording and any recent updates.

- **Minors attending engineering/DIY meetups with a parent:** No guidance found from eSafety, Meetup.com, or Australian authorities on children attending hands-on hobby meetups (e.g., drone clubs, woodworking, electronics) with parental supervision. Meetup's rule is that children can attend *any* supervised event, but no DIY-specific guidance was found.

- **Drinking culture at casual meetups:** Dating apps warn about alcohol; makerspaces ban use under the influence of drugs/alcohol. No guidance found on how to frame social responsibility around alcohol at casual in-person hobby meetups (e.g., post-workshop drinks at a pub).

- **Specific Australian community workshop safety standards:** Information comes from university labs (UNSW, RMIT, UWollongong) and public library makerspaces. No industry-wide safety standard or body was found for Australian DIY/hobby workshops or community maker spaces (e.g., is there an equivalent of a trades safety code?).

- **Australian law on meeting after online contact:** Victoria Police note increased reports of assault after meeting someone from online, but no specific legal framework or best-practice code for online-to-offline meetups in Australia was found beyond eSafety's enforcement of a dating industry code (details not fetched).
