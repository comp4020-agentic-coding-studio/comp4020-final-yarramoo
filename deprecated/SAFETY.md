# Staying safe

Makers Wanted exists so people can find each other and build things together. Most of that happens offline, with real people, in real places. Almost everyone here is just keen to make something. A few simple habits keep it that way, and they are the same habits you would use meeting anyone from the internet.

## Meeting your team

Meet somewhere public the first time, like a library makerspace or a café, and do it in daylight. Tell a friend or family member where you're going and when you expect to be home. Get yourself there and back with your own transport. A quick video call beforehand is a perfectly reasonable thing to ask for. You can leave at any point, for any reason, and you don't owe anyone an explanation.

## Garages and homes

A lot of building happens in someone's garage or spare room. Please don't share your home address until you've met in public and feel comfortable. When you do visit a home workshop for the first time, bring a friend along.

## Tools and workshop safety

If you're using someone else's tools, ask for an induction first. Wear closed shoes, tie back long hair, and skip loose clothing and dangling jewellery. Use eye and ear protection when it's called for. Only use equipment you've been shown how to use, and ask if you're not sure. Check there's a first-aid kit and that you know where it is. Rocketry has its own risks, so fly with a club: have a look at [rocketry](/c/rocketry).

## Money and scams

Never send money to someone you haven't met in person. Be wary of any request for payment, vague answers to simple questions, or someone who keeps dodging a meetup. Real collaborators rarely need your cash up front.

<h2 id="young-people">18+ and young people</h2>

Accounts on Makers Wanted are for people aged 18 and over. Young people are welcome at meetups only with their own parent or guardian present, and that parent or guardian stays responsible for them. Adults shouldn't arrange to meet young people without their parent or guardian involved.

## Trust your instincts, then report and block

If something feels off, it's fine to step away. You don't need proof or a good reason.

Every project, question, answer, progress update and profile has a small **Report** link (you won't see one on your own posts). Pick a reason, add a few details if you like, and it goes privately to the site's admin. Nobody else sees it, and the person you report isn't told.

On a member's profile you can also **Block** them. Blocking is silent: they aren't told. You'll no longer get notifications involving each other, you can't send each other join requests, and their answers and updates are folded away on pages you view. You can unblock at any time from the same place.
