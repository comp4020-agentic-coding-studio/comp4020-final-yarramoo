## Simulation bugs

### 3. Liquids stop dead at chunk borders (PRIORITY #1)

> "There appears to still be some bugs in the particle simulation. Liquids flow up to chunk borders. ...
> Fixing this will be priority #1."

Screenshots: `docs/art/playtest-2026-11-03/liquid-walls-1.png` and `liquid-walls-2.png` (from the user's Desktop, 7.18 pm and 7.19 pm).

- **What they show:** in a game world, lava and a large body of water end in straight vertical walls and
  flat-topped steps on 64-cell boundaries. They hang in mid-air instead of spreading or falling. In the
  second shot, the water's left edge is a column of stepped vertical faces.
- **Cause unknown.** Two candidates, neither measured:
  - **The active region.** `Sim::refresh_active` (`sim/src/sim.rs:331`) steps only the chunks that intersect
    a 576 x 320 rectangle around each active player (plan 6.5). Everything outside it is frozen, so liquid
    would stop at the first chunk edge outside it. **Against this:** judging by the avatar's size (about 2-3
    px per cell), the walls in both shots are only about 115-150 cells from the player, which is inside the
    rectangle's 288 x 160 half-extent. Still possible if the player had only just arrived.
  - **Chunk sleeping, or the chunk-ordered sweep.** A chunk that sleeps while its neighbour's edge column can
    still move would freeze a liquid face on a chunk boundary. Sleeping is meant to be invisible (D-054, test
    D7), and D-081/D-082 ruled it out for an earlier staircase report. But D7 compares sleeping on and off
    on scripted runs, which may not include a liquid front crossing a chunk edge next to air.
- **Dev task:**
  1. Find the cause before choosing a fix. Reproduce it with `/game?world=game&avatar=1`:
     - check with `__devMe()` whether the walls sit on the active rectangle's edge, and whether they move
       when the player moves;
     - then rerun with `set_sleep_enabled(0)`. If the walls disappear, sleeping is not invisible, which breaks
       invariant 8, and a D7 case should be written to show it red;
     - then write a native test: a liquid column next to air on a chunk boundary, run with sleeping on and off,
       asserting that it levels.
  2. If it is the active region, it's a design choice to make with the user, since the region is consensus
     state and bounds per-tick work. Options include:
     - a bigger region;
     - a wider band around it where only gravity runs (falling and liquids, no reactions);
     - letting a chunk that holds moving liquid stay awake outside the region, up to a cap;
     - waking the chunks just outside the region when it moves, so frozen fronts settle as the player
       nears;
     - drawing frozen chunks dimmed, so the freeze reads as fog rather than a bug.
     If it is sleeping, there's no design choice: it's a bug fix, and the new test proves it.
  3. Whichever it is, the fix changes every hash, so it needs a determinism suite run, a D-row, an
     engineering-decisions entry and an independent Sonnet review (it touches `sim/`).

### 4. Lone liquid droplets skitter around on flat ground

> "Small particles of liquid skit around when on a flat surface. Ideally they would stick together, and
> shouldn't be darting around without anything pushing them."

Screenshot: `docs/art/playtest-2026-11-03/lone-droplets.png` (single lava cells scattered along a flat dirt
surface).

- **Cause (read from the code, not yet shown by a test):** `Grid::liquid_move` (`sim/src/grid.rs:437`). A liquid
  cell that can't fall picks a random side every update (`rng(..., l.side)`), and `slide` moves it up to `reach`
  cells that way (water 6, oil 5, acid 4, lava 3). Nothing ever tells a lone cell on flat ground that it's done,
  so it random-walks forever, up to 6 cells per tick. That's the darting. A side effect: these cells probably
  keep their chunks awake (each move is a `touch`), which costs CPU across the whole world.
- **Dev task:** give liquids a rest state.
  - A lone cell on a flat floor, or a thin film one cell deep, should stop rather than walk. One option is to
    slide only when the cell has liquid above it, or a neighbour pushing on it (pressure), or when the slide
    reaches a drop (a gap below within `reach`).
  - Liquid cells should cling: a cell with a same-liquid neighbour on one side shouldn't slide away from it
    onto bare ground.
  - **Keep D-081/D-082 working.** Deep heaps must still level fast; the existing regression test (lava heap
    flat by about tick 16000) must stay green.
  - Must stay integer-only, counter-based rng, bounded, and identical with sleeping on and off (D7).
  - Add a native test: a single water cell and a single lava cell on a flat floor stop moving within N
    ticks, and their chunk goes to sleep. Show it red first.
  - **May be related to note 3.** If droplets and liquid fronts both depend on how `slide` and sleeping interact
    at chunk edges, investigate the two together. Same gate: determinism suite, a D-row and a sim review.

## Feel

### 13. The walking animation looks jittery

> "The walking animation looks quite jittery. Perhaps moving between frames every tick?"

- **What the code does (read, not measured):** `updatePose` (`client/fx.ts:265`) advances
  `walkPhase += |vx| * ticks * 0.45`, and `spriteFor` (`client/fx.ts:196`) picks one of 4 walk frames from
  `floor(walkPhase) & 3`. At walking speed (`WALK = 0.75` cells per tick, `client/avatar.ts:18`), that's 0.34
  phase per tick: **a new frame about every 3 ticks (about 20 frames per second), so the whole 4-frame cycle
  plays 5 times a second.** Not quite every tick, but fast enough to read as a flicker. Three other things may
  add to the jitter:
  1. **Position snapping:** `pushSprite` rounds the feet to whole cells (`Math.round(px)`), and 0.75 cells per
     tick lands on a 1, 1, 1, 0 step pattern. At 2-3 px per cell, that's a visible stutter.
  2. **Remote players:** `vx` comes from the per-frame change in an interpolated position. When Pos updates
     arrive unevenly, `vx` spikes, so the frame rate jumps and the pose may flicker between walk, idle and
     jump (grounded is guessed from `vy` for others).
  3. Frame timing tied to `dt`, so a frame-rate hitch skips frames.
- **Dev task:** client-only, no sim change.
  - Slow the cycle to about 8-10 frames per second at walk speed (a factor of about 0.15-0.2 instead of 0.45),
    or step frames by distance (one frame per 2 cells walked).
  - Check whether the snapping matters by drawing the sprite at sub-cell positions (or rounding the camera
    and the sprite together).
  - For remote players, smooth `vx` (an exponential average) and add hysteresis to the walk/idle switch.
  - Look at it at 1x and 2x zoom, for your own avatar and for another player in a second browser.
