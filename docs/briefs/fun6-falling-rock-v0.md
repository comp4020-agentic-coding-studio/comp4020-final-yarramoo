# Brief: falling rock v0, cut-free islands crumble

**Why:** the user asked about rigid bodies (31 Oct). The research (`docs/research/rigid-bodies.md`,
engineering-decisions entry 28) recommends **v0** as cheap and safe: detect a solid island that has been cut
free and crumble it into a falling powder. It gives most of the visible "the chunk falls" effect, needs no new
entity type and **no snapshot format change**. Read `docs/research/rigid-bodies.md` sections 1, 5, 6 and 7.

**Worker:** one Sonnet worker. Use no subagents, and stay within **at most about 55 tool calls** (the budget
is tight tonight). Read `CLAUDE.md` first: the sim invariants are binding (no floats, counter-based rng with a
new salt, **bounded work per tick**, **chunk sleeping invisible**, `rm` only on literal absolute paths, every
commit gated on exit codes chained with `&&`, every new test red first, no pushing).

## What to build (`sim/src/grid.rs`, `tuning.rs`, `rng.rs`)

1. **Seeds.** When a solid cell (Rock, Dirt, Coal, Ice, Glass, Wood) becomes non-solid through any path
   (`dig`, `carve`, acid dissolving, the Black Hole, a worm, an explosion, Paint), record the up-to-4 solid
   neighbours as **seeds** in a bounded per-tick queue (`MAX_ISLAND_SEEDS`, for example 32; extra seeds are
   dropped). The cleanest hook is wherever the grid writes a cell; see `Grid::set`. Don't hook every `set`
   call site.
2. **Detection, once per tick, bounded.** For each seed, flood-fill solid cells (4-connected) up to
   `ISLAND_MAX` cells (for example 400).
   - If the fill reaches the cap, **or touches Bedrock, or touches the world edge**, the island is
     **anchored**: nothing happens. A cut-free ceiling therefore stays put; that's the research's anchored
     rule.
   - Otherwise it's a free island.
   - Bound the total flood work per tick (`ISLAND_WORK_MAX`, for example 4000 cell visits). Seeds past the
     budget wait for the next tick, in queue order, so the result stays deterministic.
   - Use a reusable scratch buffer, not a `HashMap` or `HashSet`: a `Vec` visited stamp or a sorted `Vec`.
3. **Crumble.** Every cell of a free island becomes a falling powder: **Sand** for Rock, Dirt and Glass;
   Coal stays Coal but must fall, so either convert it to Sand or add no new material and keep it as Sand
   (pick one and flag it); Ice becomes Water; Wood becomes Sand or stays (flag it). The simplest honest v0:
   everything crumbles to Sand except Ice, which becomes Water. Write through `Grid::set` so `touch` wakes
   the chunks.
4. **Sleeping (D7).** Detection runs from seeds generated by writes, which happen identically on every
   client. It must not depend on `awake`. Only enqueue from cells inside **active** chunks, or prove that a
   seed in a frozen chunk is harmless. Either way, **sleeping on or off must give the same hash.**

## Tests (each red first)

- A floating 5x5 rock block, freed by digging its last connection, crumbles to sand that falls.
- A block still attached to anything anchored doesn't crumble.
- A block bigger than `ISLAND_MAX` doesn't crumble (anchored by the cap).
- A block touching Bedrock doesn't crumble.
- **Bounded work:** many seeds in one tick keep the total flood visits at or below `ISLAND_WORK_MAX`. Show
  the bound bite: halve it, and a test that needs the full budget fails, or assert the exact visit count.
- **D7:** a freed island near a player, run with sleeping on and off, gives equal hashes.
- **The generated world is stable:** run 600 ticks of a fresh seed-1 Survival world with a player standing
  still, and nothing crumbles. The generator must not leave free islands behind, or the world would
  collapse at start. If it does leave some, either crumble them in generation or exclude generation-time
  cells from seeding, and say which.
- `traversable_to_every_band` stays green.
- **The determinism suite** (Node and the three browsers) passes. The D2 scripts already dig and bomb.
